Strumprint Studio

Strum a song, get its print: notes, tab and the shape of its key, written out as you play.

What it is

Strumprint Studio listens to your guitar through an audio interface or a microphone and writes down what you play, live. You see each note, the tab on the easiest strings and frets, and where your playing sits on the circle of fifths, with the chords that fit your key.

It runs in your web browser. Nothing is installed and nothing you play leaves your computer.

The idea

Every player leaves a print. Sound is vibration, and vibration has shape: scatter sand on a vibrating plate and it gathers into patterns called cymatics. The title shows this. Its sand forms the name, shakes into one of five fingerprint patterns, then breaks into waves that follow your notes.

The colors come from lava meeting oxidized copper. Sound is the heat; notes cool from molten orange into patina teal, and each of the twelve notes keeps its own color everywhere in the studio.

How it works

  • Hearing notes: the McLeod Pitch Method finds the pitch of each sound, many times a second.
  • Writing tab: each note is placed on the string and fret closest to where your hand already is.
  • Finding the key: the notes you play are compared with the Krumhansl–Schmuckler key profiles, a classic model of how strongly each note belongs to each key.

Free and open

The code is public domain under the Unlicense: anyone can use, change or share it for anything. Chord shapes and recordings from the community are released under CC0, so the library stays free for everyone too.

Want to help? Add a chord shape or record a chord with the simple method in the project's CONTRIBUTING.md and recordings/README.md.
